Approved senders list SPF

Names the only service allowed to send email as us. The -all at the end means: everyone else is not allowed.

v=spf1 include:spf.brevo.com -all

Digital signature DKIM

Every real email we send carries a signature that only our sending service can make. A stranger can copy our name, but not the signature.

brevo1._domainkey → b1.almindhrybuilds-me.dkim.brevo.com brevo2._domainkey → b2.almindhrybuilds-me.dkim.brevo.com

03

The instruction to Gmail and Outlook DMARC

p=reject tells them to refuse any email that claims to be from us and fails the two checks above. Strict mode (adkim=s, aspf=s) means it must match our exact address, not just a similar one.

v=DMARC1; p=reject; adkim=s; aspf=s; rua=mailto:rua@dmarc.brevo.com

What we learned doing it

Three traps, and how we avoid them on your email.

Trap 1

Setup wizards suggest “watch only”.

Ours did too, while we were already on “refuse”. Copying it would have switched our protection off. We kept refuse and added the wizard's reporting address instead.

Trap 2

Two approved-senders lists cancel each other.

Add a second list for a new tool and both stop working. There must be exactly one, with every real sender on it.

Trap 3

Going straight to “refuse” can block your own invoices.

We have one sending service, so we could. Most companies have several: mailbox, website form, accounting software. So yours goes in stages: watch, then spam folder, then refuse.
