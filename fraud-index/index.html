What stands out

Most companies switched it on, then stopped.

Almost two in five have the setting in place but on “watch only”. They did the first step and never finished, so fake emails still reach their customers.

4%

fully refuse fake emails: 20 of 537 companies. That's the only setting that stops a fake invoice before anyone sees it.

39%

are on “watch only”: 207 companies. Switched on, but asking Gmail and Outlook to do nothing.

7%

don't even list which servers may send their email (no SPF record): 38 companies.

How we measured it

  • Who: 537 companies with a website and working email, found through Google Maps business listings in Oman, the UAE and Tanzania between October and November 2026. Trading, logistics, accounting, clinics, schools, travel and more. Country comes from the listing's phone number.
  • What: each company's public DNS settings, read through Google Public DNS on 21 November 2026: the DMARC policy (none, quarantine, reject) and the SPF record. Anyone can read these settings; we accessed nothing private.
  • “Can be faked” means no DMARC record, a broken one, or DMARC set to p=none. In all three cases receiving mail servers get no instruction to block a forged email.
  • Limits: this isn't a random sample of every company in each country; it leans towards small and mid-sized firms that list a website on Google Maps. The UAE sample (30) is small. We didn't measure DKIM signatures. A company may have other protections we can't see from outside.
  • Privacy: we publish totals only. No company is named, now or in future editions.
